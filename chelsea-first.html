José Mourinho at Chelsea

After winning the UEFA Champions League with FC Porto in 2004, José Mourinho arrived in England as one of the most highly regarded young managers in world football.

Chelsea appointed Mourinho in the summer of 2004. His arrival immediately created enormous expectations around Stamford Bridge. Mourinho embraced the pressure and famously introduced himself as the "Special One."

Chelsea had already become a major force under Roman Abramovich, but Mourinho's arrival transformed the club into an exceptionally consistent winning machine.

2004–05: Ending the 50-Year Wait

Mourinho's first season in English football became historic almost immediately.

Chelsea started the 2004–05 Premier League season with an extremely organised and disciplined team. The defence, led by John Terry and Ricardo Carvalho, was supported by Claude Makélélé in midfield and Petr Čech in goal.

The team became famous for its defensive strength. Chelsea conceded only 15 goals throughout the entire Premier League season and recorded 25 clean sheets. They finished with 95 points.

On 30 April 2005, Chelsea defeated Bolton Wanderers 2–0 away from home, with Frank Lampard scoring both goals. The victory secured Chelsea's first English top-flight championship in 50 years.

Mourinho had delivered the Premier League title in his first season.

The League Cup

Chelsea also won the League Cup during Mourinho's first season.

In the final, Chelsea defeated Liverpool 3–2 after extra time at the Millennium Stadium.

The victory gave Mourinho his first domestic trophy in English football and demonstrated that Chelsea were capable of competing successfully on multiple fronts.

2005–06: Defending the Crown

The following season proved that Mourinho's first Premier League title was not a one-off achievement.

Chelsea again dominated the league and finished the season as champions.

They collected 91 points and finished eight points ahead of Manchester United.

Chelsea therefore became back-to-back Premier League champions, with Mourinho establishing the club as the dominant force in English football.

The 2006–07 Season

Chelsea entered the 2006–07 campaign attempting to win an unprecedented number of trophies.

Although they eventually finished second in the Premier League, Mourinho still added two major domestic trophies to his collection.

Chelsea defeated Arsenal in the League Cup final, winning the competition for the second time under Mourinho.

Later that season, Chelsea faced Manchester United in the FA Cup final at the newly rebuilt Wembley Stadium.

Didier Drogba scored the decisive goal in extra time, giving Chelsea a 1–0 victory and Mourinho his first FA Cup.

A New Era at Chelsea

Mourinho's first spell at Chelsea changed the club's place in English football.

In only three seasons, he won six major trophies and established Chelsea as one of Europe's elite clubs.

His Chelsea teams were known for their organisation, physical strength, tactical flexibility and ability to control matches.

Mourinho also developed a close relationship with many of his players. John Terry, Frank Lampard, Didier Drogba, Petr Čech, Claude Makélélé and several others became central figures of the team.

Departure

Mourinho's first Chelsea spell ended in September 2007 after the club and the manager agreed to part ways.

He left Stamford Bridge having won six major trophies in just over three years and having completely changed Chelsea's status in English football.

Legacy

Mourinho's first Chelsea spell is widely regarded as one of the most important managerial periods in the club's history.

He ended Chelsea's 50-year wait for an English top-flight title, defended it the following season, and won four domestic cup trophies during his first spell.

His success in England also cemented Mourinho's reputation as one of the world's leading managers.
