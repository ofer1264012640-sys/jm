José Mourinho at Inter Milan

After leaving Chelsea in September 2007, José Mourinho spent several months away from management before returning to the dugout with Inter Milan in the summer of 2008.

Inter had dominated Italian football domestically in the years before Mourinho's arrival, winning three consecutive Serie A titles. However, the club had struggled to reproduce that dominance in Europe.

Mourinho was brought to Milan with one major objective: to take Inter to the highest level of European football.

His appointment immediately attracted enormous attention. Mourinho was already a Champions League winner with Porto and a two-time Premier League champion with Chelsea.

Arriving in Milan

Mourinho's first press conference as Inter manager quickly became famous. His confidence, personality and ambition immediately made headlines in Italy.

However, behind the personality was a highly organised football manager who understood exactly what he wanted from his team.

Mourinho inherited an Inter squad containing experienced players such as Javier Zanetti, Esteban Cambiasso, Walter Samuel and Marco Materazzi, while Zlatan Ibrahimović was the team's main attacking star.

Mourinho also strengthened the squad with players including Sulley Muntari and Ricardo Quaresma.

2008–09: Winning Serie A

Mourinho's first season in Italy began with Inter competing for the Italian Super Cup.

Inter defeated Roma on penalties to win the trophy, giving Mourinho his first piece of silverware in Italian football.

The Serie A campaign was highly competitive. Inter eventually established themselves at the top of the table and finished the season as Italian champions.

Mourinho had won the Serie A title in his first season, extending Inter's domestic dominance.

The Champions League campaign, however, ended in the round of 16. Inter were eliminated by Manchester United, the reigning European champions.

Mourinho knew that domestic success alone would not be enough. The ultimate goal was still European glory.

Building a European Champion

During the summer of 2009, Inter made several important changes to the squad.

Zlatan Ibrahimović left for Barcelona, while Samuel Eto'o arrived in the opposite direction.

Inter also signed Wesley Sneijder from Real Madrid and brought in Diego Milito and Thiago Motta from Genoa.

These changes proved crucial. Mourinho created a team that was tactically flexible, physically powerful and extremely difficult to break down.

The 2009–10 squad would eventually become one of the most famous teams in Inter's history.

2009–10: The Historic Season

Inter began the 2009–10 season with one objective: to win everything.

The team once again competed for the Serie A title, while simultaneously fighting for the Coppa Italia and Champions League.

Mourinho's tactical approach became particularly effective in the biggest matches.

Inter were capable of defending extremely deeply when necessary, but could also attack quickly through players such as Samuel Eto'o, Diego Milito and Wesley Sneijder.

Coppa Italia

Inter reached the Coppa Italia final and faced Roma.

Diego Milito scored the only goal of the final, giving Inter a 1–0 victory.

Mourinho had won his first major trophy of the 2009–10 campaign.

Winning Serie A Again

The Serie A title race went down to the final weeks of the season.

Inter eventually finished first, securing their fifth consecutive Serie A championship.

Mourinho had now won back-to-back league titles in Italy.

But the biggest challenge was still ahead.

The Battle Against Barcelona

Inter were drawn against Barcelona in the Champions League semi-final.

Barcelona were the defending European champions and were widely regarded as one of the strongest teams in the world.

The first leg was played at San Siro.

Inter produced one of the greatest performances in Mourinho's career, defeating Barcelona 3–1.

The second leg at Camp Nou was completely different. Inter were forced to defend for long periods after Thiago Motta was sent off.

Mourinho's team defended with incredible discipline and determination.

Barcelona won the match 1–0, but Inter progressed 3–2 on aggregate.

Mourinho's celebration at the final whistle became one of the most iconic images of his career.

Champions League Final

On 22 May 2010, Inter faced Bayern Munich in the Champions League final at the Santiago Bernabéu in Madrid.

Inter produced another disciplined tactical performance.

Diego Milito scored both goals as Inter defeated Bayern Munich 2–0.

Inter were European champions.

Mourinho had won the Champions League for the second time in his career.

The Treble

The Champions League victory completed one of the greatest achievements in club football.

Inter had won:

  • Serie A
  • Coppa Italia
  • UEFA Champions League

Mourinho had completed the historic treble with Inter, winning the league, domestic cup and Champions League in the same season.

Inter had achieved the treble for the first time in the club's history.

Mourinho's Legacy at Inter

Mourinho's two seasons at Inter transformed his reputation once again.

He had already won the Champions League with Porto, but doing it again with Inter demonstrated that his success was not limited to one club or one league.

His Inter team became famous for its tactical discipline, defensive organisation, counter-attacking ability and mental strength.

Players such as Diego Milito, Wesley Sneijder, Samuel Eto'o, Javier Zanetti, Esteban Cambiasso, Lucio and Walter Samuel became central figures in one of the greatest teams in Inter's history.

Leaving Inter

Immediately after winning the Champions League, Mourinho announced his decision to leave Inter.

He left the club at the absolute peak of his career.

Mourinho had completed the treble and decided to take on a new challenge in Spain with Real Madrid.

His departure marked the end of one of the greatest managerial spells in Inter Milan's history.

Mourinho left Milan as a legend.
